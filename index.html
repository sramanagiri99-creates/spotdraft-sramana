§01What I noticed

There’s a lot of content. But is it building an audience?

The channel has a substantial volume of published content, but the subscriber count suggests an opportunity to rethink what makes people want to watch, return and follow SpotDraft.

Instagram

Content without a distinctive visual identity.

The feed has interviews, event photographs, product content, text-led creatives and promotional posts. There are good individual ideas, but they don’t consistently come together through one visual language or recognisable brand voice.

The bigger gap

SpotDraft has the product, the industry expertise and the stories.

What I see as an opportunity is to connect them through a more distinctive and consistent content identity.

§02What I’d do differently

Make legal technology interesting, not just informative.

Rather than relying heavily on product announcements, event updates and conventional educational posts, I’d introduce recurring content concepts that make people curious about contracts and the problems surrounding them.

Seriesi.

The Contract That…

Short, story-led content built around situations people can relate to.

  1. Ep. 01The contract that almost cost a company a deal.
  2. Ep. 02The renewal nobody remembered.
  3. Ep. 03The clause that changed everything.
  4. Ep. 04The agreement that took three weeks to get signed.

Make contract-related problems feel like real stories, not software explanations.

Seriesii.

Legal vs Business

A creative series showing the different perspectives of legal and business teams.

The askMeanwhile Sales wants the deal signed today.Legal wants another review. Finance needs the agreement.Nobody knows where it is. The founder wants to close the deal.The contract is still stuck in approvals.

Humour, relatable situations and short-form storytelling that make the content shareable.

Seriesiii.

Clause of the Week

Take one interesting contract clause and explain it.

  1. i.What it actually means
  2. ii.Why it matters
  3. iii.Where it can affect a business
  • Simple visual explainers
  • Short videos
  • Real-world examples

One clause a week, explained in plain language.

Seriesiv.

Behind the Contract

Bring out the people and expertise behind SpotDraft.

  • Conversations with legal professionals
  • Customer stories
  • Founder perspectives
  • How modern legal teams work

The people who make contracts work, in their own words.

The intention is to build recurring formats that audiences begin to recognise and look forward to.

§03Build a recognisable content identity

Not every post should look like it came from a different brand.

I’d establish a clear creative direction across Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn. This means:

  1. A consistent visual system for typography, colours, layouts and thumbnails.
  2. A recognisable tone of voice across all content.
  3. Better scripting, audio, editing and presentation for video.
  4. Distinct formats for different platforms rather than simply reposting the same content everywhere.
  • YouTubeDepthBuild deeper engagement and thought leadership.
  • InstagramDiscoveryMake the brand more discoverable and relatable.
  • LinkedInCredibilityEstablish expertise and credibility.

Beyond own channels

Collaborations with legal professionals and industry voices

Partnering with respected voices puts SpotDraft in front of audiences who already trust them. The biggest reach multiplier sits inside the company, which is the next point.

The focus would be on creating content that people find useful, interesting or worth sharing, not just content that fills a calendar.

§05Employee-led content on LinkedIn

People follow people. Let SpotDraft’s team carry the brand on LinkedIn.

SpotDraft’s legal experts, sales team, product people and founders already live the contract problems customers face. Posts from their own profiles reach networks a company page never will, and buyers trust a practitioner more than a logo. Two B2B companies show how far this goes.

Case study

Clay

Data enrichment and GTM platform

6MLinkedIn impressions in one quarter from executive content
~80Knew followers for the executives who wrote those posts
  • Each buyer group (GTM engineers, revenue leaders, founders) was matched to an executive who does that job.
  • The company page handles launches and news, so people write about their own work, not announcements.
  • One owner runs a weekly rhythm and follow-ups, because getting people to post is the hard part.

Figures shared by Clay’s Head of Marketing, Bruno Estrella (May 2026), via Supergrow.

Case study

ColdIQ

Outbound and GTM agency

24employees posting, 581 posts in under 90 days
+34Knew followers across their personal profiles
250K+combined team audience
+$151Knew MRR from 27 clients, as reported by ColdIQ
  • A quarterly internal competition with cash prizes, plus a bonus for 20+ posts.
  • Coaching on hooks and planning, with designers and tools that cut the effort of posting.
  • The founder, Michel Lieben, posts first and sets the pace.

Self-reported by ColdIQ: How we turned 24 employees into LinkedIn influencers.
